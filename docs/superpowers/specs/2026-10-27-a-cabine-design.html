1Intent and the why

Mathias's friend has a Pioneer DDJ-FLX4 at home. His train ride home is dead time he wants to turn into practice. Asked what a good practice ride should leave him better at, the answer was "familiarity and reps": not lessons, not scores, just time with his hands on the board.

So the one thing that matters is transfer. Whatever his thumbs learn on the phone should still be true on the FLX4: where play and cue sit, what SHIFT plus a button does, how the jog nudges, which way the CFX knob goes for a low-pass. Every choice in this doc is judged against that. A nicer phone-native DJ app would be a failure here.

It lives in the mesa because the mesa already has the hard parts he needs around the board: a crate with BPM and key analysis, YouTube and Spotify intake, a PIN gate, and O Arquivo for recorded mixes.

2Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals

  • Both decks and the mixer on one landscape phone screen, in FLX4 positions.
  • Two songs playing at once, mixed live with jog, tempo, EQ, filter, faders, crossfader.
  • Controls behave like an FLX4 running rekordbox, including SHIFT combos.
  • Works with no signal for the whole ride once tracks are carried.
  • He can record a ride's mix and it ends up in O Arquivo.
  • He fills his crate with techno himself, from the phone.

Non-goals (v1)

  • Lessons, scores, provas, streaks.
  • Real FLX4 over USB/Web MIDI (a later idea; the mesa already has a MIDI path).
  • Mic, Smart CFX, Smart Fader, PAD FX, KEYBOARD, KEY SHIFT behavior.
  • Serato behavior or a Serato mode.
  • Key lock (master tempo). Tempo moves pitch like vinyl in v1.
  • Portrait layout. Portrait shows a "turn sideways" card.

Anti-goals

  • Any control in a different spot, or doing something different, than on the FLX4.
  • Phone-only shortcuts that do not exist on the hardware (tap waveform to play, auto-sync on load, swipe gestures on the board).
  • A mix dying in a tunnel because something needed the network.
  • Breaking or slowing the existing mesa page, its crate, or its endpoints.
  • Secrets or the PIN in the public repo.

5Control map

Behavior is rekordbox's with an FLX4 attached, as he would feel it at home. Touch translations are the only liberty taken, and each is listed. Every control must support multi-touch independently (two thumbs on two different controls at once, plus SHIFT held).

The manual wins.The SHIFT combos and edge behaviors below were written from memory of the DDJ-400/FLX4 docs. The builder checks each one against the DDJ-FLX4 operating instructions for rekordbox before building it; where this table and the manual disagree, follow the manual and note the change.

Deck (x2, mirrored as on the hardware)

Waveform strip (the "laptop screen")

Two horizontal scrolling waveforms, deck 1 on top, playhead at center, beat grid ticks, hot cue markers, loop region. Per deck: artist and title, current BPM (with tempo applied), key, time remaining, SYNC state. A thin full-track overview per deck shows position. It is read-only my call, following the anti-goals: tapping the waveform does nothing, because at home it is a laptop screen across the desk.

6Tracks: crate, grid, carry

Getting techno in

A Cabine gets a crate screen that works in portrait (the "turn sideways" card only covers the board). It reuses the mesa's intake endpoints:

  • YouTube: existing search and pull (/api/intake/youtube/search, /api/intake/pull).
  • Spotify: existing name search through the taste engine; a pick resolves to a YouTube upload he chooses, never silently. The "my Spotify" and "liked" tabs are Mathias's account and are not shown in A Cabine.
  • SoundCloud: new. Search and pull through the same downloader the intake uses (yt-dlp handles SoundCloud). The container's yt-dlp is 2026.07.04; the builder confirms it can still search and pull from both YouTube and SoundCloud before relying on it, and bumps it if not.
  • The existing crate: every analyzed track in the mesa is browsable and carryable, not only sources/techno/.

Beat grid (new)

Sync, quantized loops, beat jump, beat FX timing and the grid ticks all need a beat grid. The analyzer today stores BPM and key only. New: a grid per track (BPM plus first-beat offset, constant tempo, which suits techno), computed when a track is pulled and on demand for older crate tracks, stored next to the analysis. Downbeat (which beat is "1") is a best guess, and the common failures (half or double tempo, grid off by half a beat, wrong beat 1) are fixed by hand with the grid fix on the crate screen (see section 3). A track whose beats are too irregular for a constant grid (the analyzer's beat_cv already measures this) is still playable but SYNC and quantize are disabled for it, with a note.

Carrying tracks

"Carry" downloads a track to the phone: stereo audio in a format the phone decodes natively, the grid, and a precomputed waveform. The crate screen shows which tracks are on the phone and how much space they use, and lets him remove them. A Cabine requests persistent storage. Carried tracks must survive the phone being offline for days.

7Recording mixes

  • A REC control sits on the waveform strip (the hardware has none, so it goes on the "screen", not the board).
  • Records the master bus only. Never the cue side, never the split headphone signal.
  • Recordings are compressed (a ride can be 40+ minutes; the mesa's current WAV recorder caps at 10 minutes and a 40-minute WAV is ~400 MB).
  • Stored on the phone first. Uploaded to O Arquivo automatically when online, retried until the server confirms. Listed as "waiting to upload" until then.
  • The server side today accepts WAV only (POST /api/mesa/export, 200 MB cap, RIFF check) and O Arquivo lists *.wav only. Both need to accept and list the compressed format, with durations, without breaking existing WAV exports.

8Constraints

The phone

The target: Mathias's iPhone.Safari on current iOS, installed to the home screen so it opens with no browser bars. iPhone web apps cannot go fullscreen or lock orientation from code, so the board relies on the home-screen install for chrome-free display and shows a "turn sideways" card in portrait. The safe areas (Dynamic Island side, rounded corners, home indicator) take about 94 px of width and 21 px of height in landscape; nothing interactive goes in them.
  • Android (second target). The friend's Galaxy in current Chrome. There, installed web apps can go fullscreen and lock to landscape, so the board does that when opened from the home screen.
  • Offline-first. Once a track is carried, nothing on the board may touch the network. The page itself must open with no signal (installed home-screen app plus an offline cache of the page and its scripts).
  • Home screen install is required, not optional. On both phones it gives the full-screen board; on iPhone, Safari can clear a website's stored data after 7 days without a visit; home-screen web apps are exempt. A Cabine should say so on first run and guide the install.
  • iPhone audio gotchas (second target) the builder must handle and prove on Mathias's iPhone: audio only starts after a tap; the ring/silent switch can mute web audio unless the page declares itself as playback audio; the screen sleeping kills playback (keep the screen awake while a deck is playing); no programmatic orientation lock on iOS.
  • Safe areas. Landscape iPhones have a notch or island on one side and the home-indicator gesture zone along the bottom. Nothing interactive in those zones; the crossfader especially must sit clear of the bottom swipe area.
  • Memory. Two decoded 10-minute stereo tracks as 32-bit float are about 420 MB. Phones kill tabs that hold that much, iPhones especially, and the risk grows with every track loaded. The builder chooses a representation that keeps two long tracks plus the sampler comfortably in budget and proves it on a phone.
  • Scrubbing and backward play. The jog's vinyl behavior needs playback at arbitrary, including negative, speeds. The standard one-shot buffer player cannot do that, so the decks need a custom player.
  • Bluetooth latency. AirPods-class headphones add roughly 150 to 250 ms. Both decks share it, so beatmatching by ear still works; jog and pad feel will lag. Nothing to fix; one line in the help says wired is tighter.
  • Touch. Multi-touch on every control at once, no page scroll, no pinch zoom, no double-tap zoom, no text selection on the board.

The server and repo

  • PIN gate. All /api/ and /audio/ paths are gated by the dj_auth cookie (30-day max age). /static/ is public, so nothing private goes there. If the cookie lapses while offline, the board must keep working with carried tracks and ask for the PIN only when it next needs the server.
  • Service worker scope. Static files live under /static/; a worker served from there would only control /static/. The worker must be served at a path whose scope covers /cabine.
  • Public repo. No PIN, hashes, tokens, or the friend's name in code, docs, or commits.
  • Do not disturb the mesa. Shared endpoints keep their current behavior for the existing page. CSS class names are checked for collisions (the mesa has been bitten by .mixer before). A Cabine's code is its own files, not more lines in escola.js (4,445 lines).
  • Host. The ryzen runs under a 2.2 GHz thermal cap. Grid analysis runs as a background job like the existing pull/analyze jobs, never in a request.
  • Deploy. dj-lab container, project dir mounted at /app. Before any restart, check /api/jobs for a live pull or separation someone else started and wait for it.

10Left to the builder

  • Audio engine architecture (deck player, FX implementations, split-cue routing), within the constraints above.
  • Phone storage mechanism, audio format for carried tracks, recording format.
  • Grid algorithm and where grids are stored, as long as old crate tracks can get one later.
  • Visual styling of the board: dark, hardware-like, legible at 844 x 390, consistent with the mesa's type where text appears. Positions are not the builder's call; they follow the hardware.
  • The exact touch math (drag sensitivity per control), tuned until fine tempo control is possible.
  • How the crate screen looks, as long as search, pull status, carry and remove are clear on a phone.
  • File layout and naming inside the repo, and tests.

11How we know it works

  • Positions: the board compared side by side with a photo of the FLX4 top panel; every control in its place.
  • Automated, desktop and phone-size browser (844 x 390 with iPhone safe areas, and 760 x 360): load two tracks, play both, drive every works control including multi-touch (SHIFT held + pad, two faders at once), and assert the audio actually changed (levels read from the audio graph, not CSS classes).
  • Sync: two tracks at different BPMs, SYNC on deck 2, measured phase drift stays under a few milliseconds over 2 minutes.
  • Offline: carry two tracks, cut the network, reload the installed page, mix and record for several minutes, restore the network, the recording lands in O Arquivo and plays from the mesa page.
  • Split cue: left channel carries only cue/MIX content, right channel only master, verified by rendering.
  • Mesa regression: the existing /mesa page, its exports, and O Arquivo behave exactly as before.
  • Real phones, early and late: the audio engine is proven on a real phone before the board is built on it (a bare test page: two long tracks loaded, both playing, scrubbing, no crackle). At the end, a checklist on Mathias's iPhone, then on the friend's Galaxy when he can try it: install to home screen, fullscreen landscape, screen stays on, two long tracks without a crash, smooth UI while both decks play, a full offline ride. Automated tests cannot prove these.
